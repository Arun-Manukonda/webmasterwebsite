Honest work

Academic integrity means your work honestly shows what you know. AI doesn't change that. It just creates new gray areas.

Teens themselves see a difference between using AI to learn and using it to replace their own work. In one national survey, most said research was acceptable, but far fewer said the same about essays.[2] Rules differ by class, though. Many teens say their school has no clear rules, or they don't know them.[3]

Three habits: (1) know the policy for this assignment and ask if unsure, (2) disclose any AI you used, (3) keep your drafts and notes to show your process.

Fair for everyone?

AI learns from data made by people, so it can pick up the world's unfairness. When some groups are under-represented in training data, the system often performs worse for them.

A 2019 study by the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology tested face recognition algorithms. It found that many of them had higher error rates for some demographic groups than for others.[5]

What you can do: notice when AI output stereotypes people, ask for multiple perspectives, and compare against diverse human sources.

Waypoint 3 of 4

Your data, your call

Whatever you type into an AI tool goes to a company's computers. Depending on the tool and your settings, it may be saved, read by reviewers, or used to train future models.

  • Keep out: passwords, addresses, phone numbers, ID numbers, health details.
  • Never share others' info (their messages, photos, or names) without consent.
  • Deepfakes: making fake images or video of real people, especially classmates, can be harassment and is illegal in some places.

Quick test: would you post it on a public bulletin board? If not, don't paste it into a chatbot.

Waypoint 4 of 4

AI's footprint

AI runs in huge data centers. The International Energy Agency estimates data centers used about 1.5% of the world's electricity in 2024. It expects that amount to more than double by 2030, driven largely by AI.[4]

Being a responsible AI citizen also means:

  • Disclose and cite: style guides like MLA explain how to cite AI-generated content.[11]
  • Keep a human in the loop: people should check AI's work and stay responsible for decisions.
  • Use it with purpose: don't run AI just because you can.

AI use statement: I used [tool] to brainstorm topics and quiz myself. All research, writing, and final choices are my own.
