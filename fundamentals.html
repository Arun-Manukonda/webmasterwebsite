Lesson 1

What is AI?

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the field of computer science focused on building systems that can do tasks that usually take human intelligence: recognizing images, understanding language, making predictions, and making decisions.

You probably used AI before breakfast. Your phone's face unlock, the spam filter in your email, autocorrect, map apps that predict traffic, and the "recommended for you" row on a streaming service are all powered by AI.

Artificial intelligence

The whole field: any technique that lets computers act "smart."

Machine learning (ML)

AI that learns patterns from data instead of following only hand-written rules.

Deep learning

ML that uses large neural networks with many layers. It powers image and speech recognition.

Generative AI

Deep learning models that create new content (text, images, audio, or code) such as chatbots and image generators.

Big idea

Today's AI can be impressive at specific skills and still fail at things a child finds easy. Experts debate if and when we will build "artificial general intelligence" that matches people at nearly everything.

Lesson 2

How machines learn

In traditional programming, a person writes the rules: "if the email contains these words, mark it as spam." In machine learning, we flip that around. We give the computer lots of examples and let it figure out the rules itself.

A few key terms:

  • Training data: the examples the model learns from.
  • Features: the details the model pays attention to (like the words in an email).
  • Labels: the correct answers attached to examples (like "spam" or "not spam").
  • Model: the result of training: a set of learned patterns that can make predictions.

Three ways machines learn

Supervised

Learns from labeled examples. "Here are 10,000 photos labeled cat or dog."

Unsupervised

Finds hidden groups or patterns in unlabeled data. "Sort these songs into groups that sound alike."

Reinforcement

Learns by trial and error with rewards. "Get points for winning the game."

Garbage in, garbage out

A model can only be as good as its data. If the training examples are messy, missing, or unfair, the model's predictions will be too.

Lesson 3

Neural networks & language models

A neural network is a type of model loosely inspired by the brain. It is made of layers of simple math units called neurons. Each connection has a weight, a number that controls how much one neuron influences the next.

During training, the network makes a guess, measures how wrong it was, and adjusts its weights a tiny bit to do better. Repeat that millions of times and the network gets good at the task. Networks with many layers are called deep learning.

So how does a chatbot "write"?

A large language model (LLM) is a huge neural network trained on enormous amounts of text. Its core skill is predicting the next token (a word or piece of a word) based on everything before it. It writes a whole answer by predicting one token, adding it, and predicting again. Developers then fine-tune models with human feedback so they follow instructions and respond more helpfully.

Lesson 4

What AI can't do (yet)

AI is powerful, but it has real limits. Knowing them is what separates a smart AI user from someone who just copies and pastes.

Hallucinations

Generative AI can invent facts, quotes, statistics, and even sources that look completely real.

Bias

Models learn from human-created data, so they can repeat stereotypes or treat groups unfairly.

Out-of-date knowledge

A model only knows what was in its training data, which stops at a cutoff date, unless it is connected to search.

No real-world experience

AI has never tasted food, felt nervous before a test, or seen your classroom. It works from patterns in data.

Sensitive to wording

Small changes in a question can produce very different answers.

Hidden reasoning

Large models are hard to fully explain, even for the people who build them.

You're the pilot

Think of AI as a copilot: helpful, fast, and sometimes wrong. You stay in charge of checking its work and making the final call.
