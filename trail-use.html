Pick the right tool

"Using AI" can mean very different tools. Choosing the right one, for the right job, is the first skill.

Teens most often use chatbots to search for information and to get help with schoolwork.[1] But a chatbot isn't always the best tool. For a transcript, use speech-to-text. For sources you can cite, use AI search and then open the original links.

Before you start: check your teacher's AI policy for the assignment, and the tool's age rules. Many AI services require users under 18 to have a parent's permission.

Chat assistants

Explain, brainstorm, quiz you. Can be confidently wrong.

AI search

Summaries with links. Always open the links.

Writing helpers

Grammar and clarity. Ask whether they're allowed.

AI tutors

Hints without giving the answer away.

Speech-to-text

Captions and transcripts for accessibility.

Image generators

Visual ideas. Watch consent and disclosure.

Prompting with GOAL

A prompt is your instruction to the AI. Vague prompts get vague answers. Use the GOAL method to make every prompt specific:

G

Goal: what do you want? "Help me understand photosynthesis."

O

Own context: "I'm in 9th grade and confused about where the oxygen comes from."

A

Ask for a format: "Use a cooking analogy, then 3 bullet points."

L

Limits: "Under 150 words. Don't skip steps."

Put it together:

Help me understand photosynthesis. I'm in 9th grade and confused about where the oxygen comes from. Use a cooking analogy, then 3 bullet points. Under 150 words, and don't skip steps.

✗ Weak prompt

photosynthesis

✓ GOAL prompt

Goal + your context + format + limits = an answer that fits you.

Then iterate:

  • "Simpler, please."
  • "Give me a sports example."
  • "What did you oversimplify?"

Study smarter, not shorter

About two-thirds of teens who use AI for school say it helps them understand their work, not just finish it faster. But many also use it simply to get answers.[14] The difference is how you use it.

Use the study loop: try the problem yourself first, ask AI to check or explain, fix your mistakes, then explain it back with the screen closed.

I solved this equation and got x = 4. Don't tell me the answer. Ask me questions that help me find my mistake.

Waypoint 4 of 4

Read laterally

Because AI can hallucinate, checking is part of using it. Professional fact-checkers use lateral reading: instead of staying on one page, they open new tabs and see what other trusted sources say.[9]

  • Search for every source an AI names. Does it exist? Does it say that?
  • Confirm numbers and dates with two reliable sources.
  • Be extra careful with recent events and very specific details.
